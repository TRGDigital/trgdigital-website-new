The care software market is crowded. There are dozens of systems available, ranging from basic digital record-keeping tools to comprehensive platforms covering care, medication, staffing, and compliance. Most come with compelling sales decks. Many come with long contracts.

Choosing the wrong system is an expensive mistake, not just financially, but in staff time, implementation disruption, and the risk of records gaps during a transition. Here's how to choose well.

## Start with your problems, not the software

The most common mistake in care software procurement is starting with a product and working backwards. The sales presentation shows you impressive dashboards and tells you that every other care home in your area is using it. But is it solving the problems your service actually has?

Before you speak to any vendor, write down your three biggest operational pain points. Are records incomplete? Is the rota a weekly ordeal? Are you producing compliance reports manually? Is medication administration taking too long? Are incident reports getting lost?

The right system is the one that solves those problems, even if it doesn't have the most impressive demo.

## Essential questions for any vendor

When you're evaluating a system, ask these directly:

**On implementation:**
- What does implementation actually involve, and who manages it?
- How long does it typically take from contract to go-live?
- What do you need from us (hardware, connectivity, data preparation)?
- What happens if we go live and it's not working?

**On training and support:**
- Who trains our staff, and how?
- What ongoing support is available, and at what hours?
- Is there a dedicated account manager, or a shared helpdesk?
- What does the support contract cost, and what does it include?

**On data:**
- Where is our data hosted, and what are the security certifications?
- Who owns our data?
- Can we export our complete records if we decide to leave?
- How do we migrate data from our current system (or paper records)?

**On compliance:**
- Is the system on the NHS Digital approved DSCR supplier list?
- Has it been used in a CQC inspection, and what was the outcome?
- How does the audit trail work?
- How are system updates and regulatory changes handled?

<Callout variant="warning">
Always ask about data portability before you sign. Some vendors make it contractually difficult or practically impossible to export your records if you want to leave. Vendor lock-in is a real risk in care software, your records belong to your residents, not to the software company.
</Callout>

## Red flags

**Long-term contracts with early exit penalties.** A vendor confident in their product doesn't need to lock you in. Be wary of contracts longer than two years, especially if there are punitive termination clauses.

**Vague implementation timelines.** "It varies" is not an answer. If the vendor can't tell you approximately how long implementation takes for a care home of your size, they either haven't done it enough times or they're not managing it well.

**Support only during business hours.** Care is a 24/7 operation. A medication administration issue at 11pm on a Sunday is not less urgent because it's outside business hours. Check what out-of-hours support looks like.

**No reference customers.** Ask for two or three reference customers, care homes of similar size and type that have been using the system for at least a year. If the vendor can't or won't provide them, that's a significant concern.

**The "we'll build that" response.** If your critical requirement (say, integration with your pharmacy) isn't currently in the product and the vendor's answer is "we can build that," get a signed development commitment with a specific timeline and the feature included in your contract. Verbal commitments before contract are worth nothing.

## Total cost of ownership

The per-seat or per-resident licence cost is rarely the whole story. Factor in:

- Implementation costs (often charged separately)
- Training costs
- Hardware requirements (tablets, devices)
- Ongoing support costs
- Integration costs (connecting to other systems)
- Data migration costs

Get a total cost of ownership over three years, not just the monthly licence. This makes meaningful comparison between vendors much easier.

## A note on care-sector specialism

General-purpose CRM and document management systems are sometimes repurposed for social care. Be cautious. A system built for care, with care plan templates, eMAR, dependency tooling, and CQC-aware reporting, will always fit the workflow better than a generic tool dressed up with care-specific configurations.

The sector has enough complexity and enough regulatory specificity that care-sector specialism in a software vendor is genuinely valuable. Ask every vendor what percentage of their customer base is in adult social care. The answer is instructive.
